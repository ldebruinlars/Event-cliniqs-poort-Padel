Eight seconds, one continuous shot, no cuts, no camera cuts. A padel match point.

COURT GEOMETRY. Hold this layout for all eight seconds. The camera sits low on the near side of the court, just behind and slightly to the left of the blond bearded player from the first reference. He is on the NEAR side, seen from behind and three-quarter, facing away from the camera toward the net. The net crosses the middle of the frame. The opposing player in the burgundy kit from the fourth reference is on the FAR side beyond the net, FACING THE CAMERA and therefore facing the blond player. The ball travels back and forth along the line between the two players. Both men always keep their eyes on the ball, turning their heads to track it. Never let both players look the same direction.

SECONDS 0-2. Match point, and the blond player is in trouble. The opponent, facing us across the net, drives a hard low ball over. The blond player is caught deep near the back glass, backpedalling off balance, racket trailing behind him, reaching.

SECONDS 2-4. The ball kicks high off the back glass wall behind him. He spins, finds it with his eyes, plants both feet on the blue floor and explodes upward, racket cocked high behind his head, body arched like a drawn bow.

SECONDS 4-5. THE CONTACT. At the very top of his jump the centre of the racket face meets the ball dead on. The ball compresses against the strings for one instant and a burst of lime light and paint spatter explodes outward from the point of impact. The ball fires away flat and fast, down over the net toward the far corner. This impact must be clearly visible and unmistakable.

SECONDS 5 TO 6.5. The opponent, still facing the camera, throws himself sideways into a full stretching dive, racket arm extended, and misses by a hand. The ball skids past him and kicks a spray of paint off the floor behind him.

SECONDS 6.5 TO 8. The blond player lands on the blue floor. The camera, which has been pushing in slowly and steadily the whole time, settles on a medium shot of him from the chest up. He clenches his fist, throws his head back and roars in triumph, chest heaving, eyes blazing. The point is won.

IDENTITY. The blond player keeps exactly the same face for all eight seconds: same blond wavy hair, same short beard, same jawline, same age as the first reference. Do not smooth, youthen, slim or otherwise change his face at any point, especially not as the camera moves closer. Keep his navy kit with lime green accents identical, and keep the POORT PADEL wordmark across his chest sharp, stable and readable in every frame.

STYLE. Painterly oil painting with thick visible brush strokes throughout, deep teal-green shadows, lime green rim light, stadium lights hazing through the dark air. The painted look stays constant from first frame to last, never drifting toward photorealism.
