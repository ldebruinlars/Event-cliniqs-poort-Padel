Eight seconds, one continuous shot, no cuts, no camera cuts.

SECONDS 0-2. Wide shot of the empty night padel court from the third reference. The blond bearded player from the first reference stands at the back of the court, racket held ready in his right hand, weight on his toes. Behind the net at the far end, the opposing player in the burgundy kit from the fourth reference crouches low in a defensive stance with his back to us.

SECONDS 2-4. The glowing lime ball from the second reference drops steeply out of the dark toward the player, bright trail behind it. He shuffles back, bends his knees, and pushes hard off the blue floor, rising into the air with the racket cocked high behind his head, body arched.

SECONDS 4-5. THE CONTACT. At the top of his jump the centre of the racket face meets the ball squarely. The ball compresses against the strings for one instant, a burst of lime light and paint spatter explodes outward from the point of impact, and the ball fires away flat and fast toward the far end of the court, its trail snapping straight behind it. This impact must be clearly visible and unmistakable.

SECONDS 5-8. He lands on the blue floor, the follow-through carrying his racket down across his body. At the far end the opponent lunges sideways but the ball is already past him. The camera, which has been pushing in slowly and steadily the whole time, settles on a medium shot of the player from the chest up, breathing hard, eyes still tracking the ball.

IDENTITY. The player keeps exactly the same face for all eight seconds: same blond wavy hair, same short beard, same jawline and same age as the first reference. Do not smooth, youthen, slim or otherwise change his face at any point, especially not as the camera moves closer. Keep his navy kit with lime green accents identical, and keep the POORT PADEL wordmark across his chest sharp, stable and readable in every frame.

STYLE. Painterly oil painting with thick visible brush strokes throughout, deep teal-green shadows, lime green rim light, stadium lights hazing through the dark air. The painted look stays constant from first frame to last, never drifting toward photorealism.
